# TopTicker

The home page (`site/index.html`, `choose.css`, `choose.js`) offers the **stock market**, **real estate** and the **daily news**, in that order, each with its own animated art, logo and live figures (index moves from `tape.json`, U.S. home prices and mortgage rates from `re/data.json`, and rotating big stories from `daily/news.json`), under a scrolling ticker tape and above quick links into popular pages. It can remember the choice (the logo link always shows the choice again).

The stock side is a static website showing the top 10 stocks in the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, Dow Jones, TSX (S&P/TSX Composite), TSX Venture (the 250 largest Venture companies, from the Yahoo screener), Nikkei 225, KOSPI 200 and Taiwan 50, plus buy ideas and stocks to watch in the coming week.

- `scripts/build_data.py` pulls prices, analyst ratings and market caps from Yahoo Finance (via yfinance) and writes `site/data.json`.
- `scripts/build_news.py` gathers the top news stories for each market into `site/news.json`, plus top stories from the Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, Bloomberg, Reuters, CNBC, Yahoo Finance, MarketWatch, The Economist, Forbes and Investing.com (each outlet's own RSS feed where it has one, otherwise Google News results limited to that site). The news page shows them under "Top stories" with a filter by outlet, and an "All markets" tab merges every market's stories.
- `scripts/build_history.py` saves price history for every stock (5-minute prices for 5 days, daily back to listing, thinned to weekly and monthly further back while keeping each stretch's highest and lowest close) into `site/hist/`, plus `site/trends.json` with a 30-point closing-price line per stock for each longer screener trend period. Prices are the actual traded closes, adjusted for splits but not dividends and the search list into `site/stocks.json`.
- `data/listings.csv` lists stocks the site should carry that aren't in any of the eight indexes yet, such as a new IPO (Anthropic, `ANT` on Nasdaq, listed Nov 19, 2026 at $83). Each row is the Yahoo symbol, name, exchange, currency, region, first trading day, IPO price and where the row came from (`manual` or `nasdaq`). US IPOs are added automatically (see `build_ipos.py`); add other exchanges' listings by hand. `build_history.py` adds them to the search list and saves their history from the first day (a stock with a single day of trading is kept), and `build_market.py` saves their company profile; they join the screener and other tools once they have about 30 trading days. The lookup marks them as a new listing with the IPO price and the move since, and on the first days it charts the 5-minute prices for every range, measured from the IPO price. Ranges longer than the stock's history are greyed out. Remove a row once the stock joins an index.
- `scripts/build_ipos.py` runs in every workflow run (every 15 minutes) and adds new US IPOs on its own: it reads the priced deals on Nasdaq's IPO calendar (this month and last, leaving out blank-cheque SPACs and raises under $20M), adds them to `data/listings.csv` with `source` set to `nasdaq` (the workflow saves the file back to main), puts every listing in `site/stocks.json` straight away and saves the first two weeks of prices for recent listings into `site/hist/`, so a new stock is searchable minutes after it is priced rather than after the nightly rebuild. Rows it added drop off a year after listing; rows added by hand (`manual`) stay. If Nasdaq can't be reached, nothing changes and the live search below still finds the stock.
- With the live price service on, the stock lookup and the header search also find any stock Yahoo Finance lists (the worker's `/search` route), and the lookup charts one that isn't in the site's lists from its full daily history (`/history`), so a stock that listed today can be looked up before anyone adds it. If no search knows a typed symbol yet, the drop-down offers to look it up directly, and a stock that hasn't made its first trade shows a page saying so.
- `scripts/build_market.py` saves facts on every stock in the eight indexes (price moves, volume, valuation, dividends, analyst views, next earnings date) into `site/market.json`, plus a company profile and financial figures per stock in `site/fund/`. The tool pages (watchlists and the rest) read these.
- `scripts/build_ratings.py` saves, for each stock analysts cover, the past year of rating changes by firm (upgrades, downgrades, new coverage, with price targets) and the monthly count of Strong Buy to Strong Sell ratings into `site/ratings/`, shown as "Who rates it" on the stock lookup.
- `site/` is the website (plain HTML, CSS and JS): `index.html`, the stocks-or-real-estate choice, `today.html`, the Today page (the daily brief, market news and the weekly recap with how past picks have done, on one page: `today.js` puts the recap first on weekends, `brief.js`, `news.js` and `recap.js` fill each section), `markets.html` for the top stocks, `stock.html` for the stock lookup, `watchlist.html` for watchlists and price alerts (with your own notes and target price per stock, and CSV download)  and `screener.html`, a stock screener with ready-made screens (including the market movers: gainers, losers, most active, unusual volume, gaps, 52-week highs and lows, most shorted, oversold and overbought; and chart signals, also in a Chart signal menu; `?screen=Gainers` opens one by name), 35 filters, saved screens, shareable links and CSV download (its trend column can show 1 month, 6 months, 12 months, year to date, 5 years, 10 years or all time), and `heatmap.html`, a treemap of each market grouped by sector and sized by market cap, and `backtest.html`, which replays a portfolio of up to five stocks or indexes from any start year with monthly additions and rebalancing, against an index, and `options.html`, an options strategy builder (calls, puts and shares, ten ready-made strategies, Black-Scholes premium estimates, payoff at expiry and today, break-evens, chance of profit and Greeks), and `analysts.html`, which ranks stocks by analyst rating and gap to price target and shows the consensus by sector, and `dividends.html`, which lists high-yield and steady dividend payers, upcoming ex-dividend dates and payouts that look at risk (from payout ratio, debt, earnings and price trend), with an income planner, and the lookup shows returns from 1 month to 10 years (with the yearly rate for 3 years and longer) and lists similar companies (same industry, closest in size) with a link to compare them, and the heatmap page also has a sector performance grid across periods from a day to a year, and `earnings.html`, which lists who reports earnings today, tomorrow, over the next 7 days and over the next 30 days (report time before the open or after the close, confirmed or estimated date, how the stock has done going in and what analysts expect, with a reports-per-day chart), and `plan.html`, an investment plan for a chosen amount ($10k, $20k, $50k or your own) and a target return of about 4%, 6% or 10% a year, with a suggested split across T-bills, bonds, dividend stocks and index funds, a simulated range of outcomes, 2008 and 2022 stress tests and written steps (all long-run estimates, worked out in the browser), and `compare.html`, which charts up to five stocks or indexes as % change and lines up their figures. Each market card on the Markets page says whether that exchange is open now and when it next opens or closes (regular hours, plus the New York, Toronto and Tokyo exchange holidays and U.S. early closes listed in `common.js`, which need the next year's dates added each autumn; Seoul and Taipei holidays aren't included). Every page has a market clock strip under the menu: one toggle per market (TSX, TSX Venture, S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow, Nikkei, KOSPI, Taiwan), and each one switched on shows a live countdown to that exchange's next open or close. The choice is saved in the browser. `common.js` holds the shared menu, the trading-hours table, the header's quick stock search and the watchlist store; watchlists are saved in the reader's browser (localStorage), so they stay on that device. The lookup chart can add technical indicators (SMA 20/50/200, EMA 20/50, Bollinger Bands, volume, RSI and MACD), which the browser works out from the saved closing prices and volumes. It can also overlay any of the eight market indexes, switching the chart to % change so they line up. Besides the preset ranges (1D to All), a Custom range charts any start and end date the reader picks, remembered in the browser.
- `portfolio.html` is paper trading: pretend cash (several portfolios), market, limit and stop orders at the site's latest prices, or recorded past trades to track real holdings. Positions, gains, sector allocation, value over time and trade history are kept in the browser. A check-up shows the weighted beta, forward P/E, yield and volatility, the spread by region, currency and sector, and notes on concentration.
- The stock lookup page has a Company and financials section (`fund.js`): a plain-language summary of the numbers, company profile, valuation, profit, financial health, dividends, trading figures, and the analyst rating with price-target range.
- Price alerts (rises to, falls to, or moves a set % in a day) are a section of `watchlist.html` (`alerts.js`); the 🔔 on each watchlist row picks that stock, and `watchlist.html?alert=AAPL#alerts` links straight to one. `common.js` checks them every minute on any page of the site while it is open and shows an on-page and optional browser notification.
- Chart signals (golden and death crosses, crossing the 50- and 200-day averages, MACD crossovers, 20-day breakouts and breakdowns, volume surges, RSI extremes, Bollinger squeezes) are worked out by `build_market.py` and picked in the screener's Chart signal menu.
- `calendar.js` is the week-by-week calendar, a section of `earnings.html` (earnings and the economic calendar) and of `dividends.html` (ex-dividend dates), filterable by market or your watchlists.
- `tools.html` holds calculators: position size from risk, profit and loss, average cost, TFSA versus RRSP, retirement savings and income, margin and margin-call price, recovering from a loss and currency conversion. (Option payoffs are on `options.html`, long-term growth on `plan.html` and dividend income in the Dividends income planner.)
- `about.html` ("About the data") lists the site's notes on where every figure comes from and how it's worked out, with a quick filter. The notes are in `site/about.json`, one entry per topic, written from the build scripts; update them when a data source or formula changes.
- Pages that were merged into others (`news.html`, `brief.html`, `recap.html`, `movers.html`, `signals.html`, `calendar.html`, `alerts.html`, `ask.html`) are kept as small pages that send old links and bookmarks to their new place.
- `.github/workflows/update.yml` rebuilds all the data every weekday after the US close and on Sundays, then deploys to GitHub Pages. Every 15 minutes in between, a quick run (a couple of minutes) refreshes stock and housing news, plus prices and the top 10 rankings while Asian or North American markets are open (`FAST=1 python scripts/build_data.py`, which reuses saved market caps, analyst data and dividends). Built data is kept between runs in the Actions cache, and a quick run turns into a full one when the build scripts changed or no full rebuild finished in 26 hours. Runs never overlap, and RentCast is only called by full runs, within its own weekly limit.

## Daily News

`site/daily/` is a news page for a morning read: the past 48 hours of headlines from about 90 outlets. `scripts/build_daily_news.py` builds `site/daily/news.json` in every run (full and quick, so every 15 minutes) from each outlet's public RSS feed: Yahoo, CNBC, Bloomberg, Forbes, Front Office Sports, Benzinga and dozens more world, U.S., Canadian, business, tech, science, sports, betting, entertainment, gaming, crypto, car, food and good-news outlets. Outlets without a working public feed (CNN, ESPN, The Action Network, Sports Business Journal, AP, Reuters, Politico, CTV, National Post, Toronto Star, TSN, and Yahoo Finance) are filled from Google News results limited to their site, and local stories for every U.S. state and Canadian province come from Google News searches (kept only when the headline names the place). Searches are saved in `site/daily/search-cache.json` and repeated only every hour (outlets) or two (states and provinces). Only the headline, a short summary and a picture are kept, linking to the publisher. Each story is tagged with genres (from the outlet's section and words in the headline) and with the provinces or states it names; CBC's and Global News's regional feeds carry their province. Stories from outlets that need a paid subscription are marked 🔒 Subscription and those that allow a few free stories before asking readers to sign in are marked 🔑 Sign-in, on both news pages; the outlet lists are in `site/paywall.js`. Stories that three or more outlets cover are grouped as "Everyone's talking about". The page (`daily.js`) has region, state and province tile maps, genre toggles, time windows, an outlet picker, search, trending words, a two-minute briefing, a random pick, saved stories and read tracking, all kept in the reader's browser.

## Real estate

`site/re/` is the real estate side, covering the United States, Canada and Mexico. `scripts/build_realestate.py` builds its data into `site/re/` (`data.json`, `hist.json`, `world_hist.json`, `listings.json`, `news.json`) from free public sources, each optional so one failing source doesn't stop the rest:

- **US metro areas (150 biggest):** Zillow Research CSVs: typical home value (ZHVI), rent (ZORI), homes for sale, new listings, list and sale prices, days to pending, price cuts, sale-to-list ratio, market heat index and Zillow's 12-month forecast. If Zillow moves a file, the script looks for it on Zillow's data page.
- **Canada:** Statistics Canada's New Housing Price Index by city (table 18-10-0205) and CMHC average two-bedroom rents (table 34-10-0133), plus the Bank of Canada's 5-year mortgage and policy rates (Valet API). Canada's resale figures (CREA MLS® HPI) are licensed, so they are not used.
- **Mexico:** Sociedad Hipotecaria Federal's quarterly prices by state, kept by hand in `data/realestate/mexico_shf.csv` and `mexico_shf_meta.json` because SHF only publishes PDF and Excel. Update both after each quarterly release.
- **All three:** the BIS house price index for each country (through DBnomics, with FRED as a backup), exchange rates from the Bank of Canada, and FRED for US mortgage rates, Case-Shiller and other US series. FRED's public download sometimes stalls for GitHub's servers; a free FRED API key (fred.stlouisfed.org, *My Account > API Keys*) added as the repo secret `FRED_API_KEY` makes it reliable.
- **States and provinces:** Zillow's state home value file for every US state, and the New Housing Price Index for each Canadian province.
- **World (about 60 countries):** the BIS residential property price index (nominal and real), central bank policy rates and US dollar exchange rates, all from the BIS data portal with DBnomics as a backup, plus the OECD's standardised price-to-income and price-to-rent ratios where it has them. These feed an investor score (0 to 100) built from real price momentum, 10-year real growth, value, the change in interest rates and the currency. Countries under sanctions are shown but not scored (`NOT_INVESTABLE`).
- **News:** Google News searches on each country's housing market.

Pages: `re/index.html` (overview: country cards, rates, top 10 lists, buy ideas, markets to watch, a three-country price chart, the top five world markets and a by-state-and-province bar chart for all three countries), `re/world.html` (world rankings: top 10 by investor score or any of 12 measures, region filters, a price chart in local currency, after inflation or in US dollars, a momentum-versus-peak map, a year-by-year heat table and every country in a sortable table), `re/markets.html` (every market in a filterable table with CSV download), `re/market.html` (one market: history chart, supply and speed, a buyer's-or-seller's gauge, own-versus-rent, similar markets and links to the listing sites), `re/listings.html`, `re/compare.html`, `re/tools.html` (mortgage payment, affordability and rent-versus-buy for each country) and `re/news.html`. `re/re-common.js` holds the shared menu, market search, table and chart.

**Investor sections of the overview.** `scripts/build_re_invest.py` builds `site/re/invest.json` in full runs: key lending rates for the US (New York Fed funds target and SOFR, prime, Freddie Mac mortgages, Treasury yields), Canada (Bank of Canada policy rate, CORRA, posted and actually-charged mortgage rates, commercial mortgage and business loan rates, prime, bond yields) and Europe (ECB policy rates, €STR, Euribor, euro area and per-country mortgage rates, business loans, the AAA euro yield, and the Bank of England's Bank Rate and UK mortgage rates); REIT returns and yields by property type for the US, Canada and Europe (Yahoo Finance); US construction spending by type (Census Bureau `privsatime.xlsx`); Canadian building permits by type for the country and every census metropolitan area (Statistics Canada table 34-10-0292 through its web service); and BIS commercial property prices. With `FRED_API_KEY` set it also adds jumbo and FHA mortgage rates, Baa bond yields, US commercial real estate loan delinquency and banks' lending standards. The overview (`re/invest.js`) shows them as the lending rates panels, a "where investors are looking and spending" heat map (filter by region, or pick the US, Canada or Mexico to see cities and states, or switch to property types), property type cards, a health check and construction charts. Cap rates, vacancy and rent growth by property type have no free feed, so they are rounded ranges from public market reports kept by hand in `data/realestate/asset_classes.json`, with the period they are from (`asOf`) and the date checked; update them when new reports come out.

**Listings.** MLS, REALTOR.ca and Mexican portal listings are licensed and can't be copied onto the site, so the listings page links out to them. For real US listings, sign up for a free [RentCast](https://www.rentcast.io/api) API key and add it as the repo secret `RENTCAST_API_KEY`. The free plan allows 50 requests a month, so the build fetches 10 big US cities once a week and saves them in `data/realestate/listings.json`. Every request is logged in `data/realestate/rentcast_usage.json` before it is sent, and the build never makes more than 40 in any 31 days, whatever day RentCast's billing month starts, so the site can't go over the free limit even if runs fail, overlap or are started by hand; check RentCast's terms before making the site public with them. Without a key, and for Canada and Mexico, the page shows homes clearly marked **Sample**, priced from each market's real typical value.

## Sharing and search

Every page has share-preview tags (Open Graph and Twitter), so a link pasted into iMessage, Slack, X or Facebook shows a picture card: `site/img/share-stocks.png`, `share-homes.png`, `share-news.png` and `share-home.png`. `site/robots.txt` and `site/sitemap.xml` help search engines find the pages (add new pages to the sitemap), and `site/manifest.webmanifest` with the PNG icons lets phones add the site to the home screen. On screens wider than a phone, a sun/moon button in the header (`site/theme.js`) switches between light and dark; the choice is saved in the browser, and phones stay dark. `site/404.html` is shown for any missing address; a link like `/AAPL` goes straight to that stock.

## When a data source fails

Yahoo, Google News and the housing sources sometimes refuse requests or send back nearly empty answers.
Every build step now keeps the last good data in that case instead of publishing the gap: an index whose
prices didn't come back keeps its last list (marked "as of" on the Markets page), stocks Yahoo skipped keep
their last facts, and news boxes keep their recent stories. Each step has its own time limit, so one slow
source can't stop the rest of the run from publishing.

After the build, `scripts/check_site.py` checks each data file's age and size and writes `status.json`
(see https://www.topticker.ca/status.json). If data stays out of date for more than one run, or a step fails
three runs in a row, it opens a GitHub issue titled "Site health: data is not updating", assigned to the
repository owner (GitHub emails you), and closes it when things recover. Pages also show a short notice
when their data is older than it should be.

## Rates, ETFs and crypto, earnings, insiders and the economy

- `scripts/build_macro.py` (every run) saves `site/macro.json`: the VIX, US 10-year yield, oil, gold, the US dollar index, USD/CAD and Bitcoin, shown as a strip on the Markets page (`macro.js`), at the end of the ticker tape and in the Daily Brief. It also saves `site/funds.json` for `funds.html` (ETFs and Crypto): about 50 popular US and Canadian ETFs listed in the script (price moves, fee, yield, size, top 10 holdings and sectors) and the 10 biggest cryptocurrencies from Yahoo's crypto screener, stablecoins left out. Fund profiles and the coin list refresh in full runs (`FULL=1`); prices every run.
- `scripts/build_econ.py` (every run) saves `site/econ.json` from Nasdaq's public economic calendar: the past week and next two for the US, Canada, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, the euro zone, Germany, the UK and China, with forecast, previous and actual figures. Market-moving releases (rate decisions, CPI, jobs, GDP...) are flagged. Shown on the Calendar page's Economy tab and in the Daily Brief.
- `scripts/build_ratings.py` now also saves each stock's last four quarters of earnings against estimates (with the move on the day the market reacted, found from trading volume in `site/hist/`) and its insider trades, in the same `site/ratings/` files, plus two summaries: `site/surprises.json` (the Earnings page's "Recent results") and `site/insiders.json` (`insiders.html`: biggest buys and sales, cluster buys and a 6-month tally). The stock lookup shows both under "Company and financials".
- `scripts/build_recap.py` (full runs) saves each week's buy ideas, sell ideas and stocks to watch once, in `data/picks/<Monday>.json` (committed by the workflow, so the record is kept), and writes `site/recap.json` for the Today page's weekly recap and the Markets page's "How past buy ideas have done" (`picks.js`): the week in markets, movers, sectors, rates, earnings reactions and how the last 12 weeks of picks have done against their markets.

## Stock pages for search engines

`scripts/build_stock_pages.py` (full runs) writes `site/stocks/<ticker>.html` for every stock, ETF and coin: the stock lookup page with its own address, title, description, canonical link and structured data, and the key facts already written in, so search engines see them without running scripts. The page's script then draws the live chart as usual (`stock.js` reads the ticker from `<body data-t>`). Links across the site (`UI.stockUrl`) point at these pages; a stock without one yet falls back to the lookup through `404.html`. The sitemap lists them.

## Explain this stock, push alerts and accounts

These run on the live price service (the Cloudflare Worker, see `worker/extras.js`):

- **Explain this stock** (button on the lookup's Company and financials): with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set, Claude writes a short plain-language take from the site's saved figures for that stock. Each stock's answer is cached for 12 hours, and each visitor is capped at 6 requests a minute and 60 a day. To switch it on, set up the live price service, create an API key at console.anthropic.com and add it as the repo secret `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; the next full run stores it in the Cloudflare Worker. Delete the secret (and the Worker's own `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` secret) to switch it off.
- **Push alerts** (Alerts page): the browser registers `site/sw.js` and a push subscription, and its active alerts are stored by the service. A cron trigger checks them against Yahoo prices every 5 minutes and sends an empty push; the service worker then asks which alerts fired and shows them, so no message encryption is needed. The push keys are made by the service on first use. iPhones and iPads need the site added to the home screen first.
- **Accounts** (Account page): no email or password. The service makes a random account key; the browser keeps watchlists, notes, alerts, paper portfolios, saved screens, chart drawings and a few settings in step with it (`UI.Account` in `common.js`), newest change of each item winning. Only a hash of the key is stored.

Both push alerts and accounts are stored in one Durable Object (`Store`, SQLite-backed, which the free Workers plan includes), declared in `worker/wrangler.toml` with the cron trigger. The **Edit Cloudflare Workers** API token covers both.

## Live prices

GitHub Pages can only serve files, so live prices come from a small Cloudflare Worker in `worker/` that fetches quotes from Yahoo Finance and caches them for 20 seconds. The pages (`site/live.js`) ask it for fresh prices every 30 seconds (main page) or every minute (stock lookup) while the tab is open. Rankings, buy and sell ideas and longer price history still refresh with the daily build. Some exchanges' prices reach Yahoo up to 20 minutes late.

To turn it on (free Cloudflare plan is enough):

1. Sign up at cloudflare.com, open **Workers & Pages** once so your `workers.dev` subdomain is created, and copy your **Account ID** from that page.
2. Create an API token at **My Profile > API Tokens > Create Token** using the **Edit Cloudflare Workers** template.
3. In this repo's **Settings > Secrets and variables > Actions**, add the secrets `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`.
4. Run the **Update data and deploy** workflow. It deploys the worker and points the site at it.

To use a price service hosted somewhere else, set the repo variable `LIVE_API_URL` to its address instead. Without either, the site shows the saved prices from the last build.

Ranking: momentum from 3, 6 and 12-month returns (40%), analyst consensus pulled toward neutral when few analysts cover a stock (30%), market cap (15%), steadiness or low volatility (15%). Stocks with no trade in a week and implausible price targets are left out. Penny stocks (under $1 USD equivalent or under $300M market cap) are excluded. Big Asian companies often trade below $5 a share, so a $5 cutoff would drop blue chips.

Run locally:

```
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/build_data.py
python -m http.server -d site
```

Not financial advice.

## Search engines

`site/robots.txt` lets every crawler in and points to `sitemap.xml`. Each deploy runs `scripts/build_sitemap.py`, which lists every page plus one stock lookup per stock (`stock.html?t=AAPL`) and one housing market lookup per market (`re/market.html?id=...`), each with the day's date. The lookup pages set their own title, description and canonical link once they load a stock or market. To prove ownership to Google Search Console or Bing Webmaster Tools with the "HTML tag" method, add the code (the `content="..."` value only) as the repository variable `GOOGLE_SITE_VERIFICATION` or `BING_SITE_VERIFICATION`; the next deploy puts the tag on the home page. A DNS record on topticker.ca works too and needs no change here.
